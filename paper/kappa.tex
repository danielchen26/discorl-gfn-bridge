\author{Tianchi Chen}

\begin{abstract}
DiscoRL replaces a hand-written reinforcement learning update with a
meta-learned one, and the meta-parameters of its strongest rule, Disco103, are
public. That makes a question askable that could not be asked of a rule nobody
had written down: \emph{is the discovered update the gradient of anything?} The
question is not rhetorical. By the Poincar\'e lemma a vector field is a gradient
exactly when its Jacobian is symmetric, so ``which objective does this rule
minimise'' is well posed only if a single scalar, $\kappa = \lVert \sym
J\rVert_F / \lVert J \rVert_F$, equals one.

We measure it. Working in prediction space rather than parameter space, the
non-conservative part of the field is isolated in the bootstrap operator
$B = \partial \hat p / \partial u$ --- precisely the term \texttt{stop\_gradient}
discards --- and we estimate $\kappa(B)$ by paired Hutchinson probes through a
single forward map. Two things make the number readable. First, an analytic
reference: a \emph{causal} bootstrap, whose target reads only the future, is
strictly triangular, so $\tr(B^2) = 0$ and $\kappa = 1/\sqrt 2$ exactly, in any
dimension. Second, an estimator validated before it is believed, on synthetic
fields spanning $\kappa \in [0.025, 1]$ and again in the narrow band where the
answer lands, and returning exactly $1.0000$ for a genuine gradient.

Across six configurations --- two environments, one of them from Disco103's own
meta-training family, and training lengths spanning a factor of sixteen ---
$\kappa(B) = 0.7084$ to $0.7094$, against a floor of $0.70711$. Disco103's update
is therefore not the gradient of any functional, and it carries no more
circulation than causal bootstrapping forces. It is also, and this matters for
how the first sentence should be read, barely non-conservative: the bootstrap
term is $1.1\%$ of the update field in Frobenius norm, so the antisymmetric
share of the whole update is $0.77\%$. The obstruction is structural and small,
and reporting either half alone misleads. Three probes of what the
discovered predictions represent all return nothing, against a positive control
that works. We report the traps that silently return the wrong answer, and the
claims of our own that we withdrew.

\medskip
That answer makes a second question askable. Tiapkin et al.\ prove that a
GFlowNet is exactly the soft-optimal policy of an explicit MDP --- interior
rewards $\log P_B$, terminal reward $\log R$, entropy coefficient one --- so
``meta-learn a GFlowNet update rule'' is a well-posed plan and not an analogy.
We check the reduction on a hypergrid and it holds to $10^{-16}$, including over
fifty random backward policies. The MDP it produces then defeats Disco103: the
rule stops at the source, $4.285$ below the achievable return, while the same
rule, network, observation and budget solve the same grid to within $0.552$ once
the per-step $\log P_B$ payments are removed. Potential shaping by a reward-free
geometric potential recovers most of that, to a gap of $0.323$, and provably does
not move the target: the soft-optimal policy moves by $1.776 \times 10^{-15}$.
Asking which tempered family the resulting sampler belongs to returns no verdict,
for a reason worth reporting: the best-fit residual is predicted by how far a
seed got from the achievable return at $R^2 = 0.993$ and extrapolates to $0.0410$
at zero gap, inside a measured ceiling of $0.0861$. At the best-optimising seed
the sampler is inside both candidate families at once, about five times colder
than the coefficient that would make it a GFlowNet. What does separate rules is
cheaper than a new architecture. Changing one scalar in the meta-objective, from
the return to $V_1 = \log Z - \KL(q \Vert P_B)$, moves a meta-learned local rule
from $\KL$ $1.7035$ to $0.0666$ on held-out landscapes, a factor of $26$ with
architecture, inputs, inner horizon and seeds identical. At matched budget that
is about nineteen times closer than detailed balance; given thirteen times more
steps detailed balance is about six times closer than the rule. It is a result
about speed, not about asymptotic accuracy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A reinforcement learning algorithm is usually an equation. DiscoRL
\cite{oh2025disco} replaces the equation with a network: a meta-network reads a
trajectory and emits targets, the agent regresses onto them, and the
meta-network itself is trained by meta-gradient across a population of agents.
The resulting rule, Disco103, matches or beats hand-designed algorithms on
Atari, ProcGen and DMLab, and generalises to environments never seen during
discovery. Its meta-parameters ship under an open licence.

That artefact makes a structural question concrete. Hand-designed rules come
with a story about what they optimise --- policy gradient ascends expected
return, trajectory balance \cite{malkin2022tb} descends an explicit residual.
A discovered rule comes with no such story. It is natural to ask which objective
it implicitly minimises, and a good deal of theory would follow if one could
name it.

The question presupposes that an objective exists. It need not. Temporal
difference learning famously is not the gradient of any function: the target is
held fixed when the gradient is taken, and the discarded term is what a genuine
gradient would have included \cite{baird1995residual,sutton2009gtd}. DiscoRL is
built the same way --- produce a target, regress onto it --- so the
presupposition has to be tested rather than assumed.

By the Poincar\'e lemma the test reduces to one scalar. On a simply connected
domain a smooth field $v$ is $-\nabla \Phi$ for some $\Phi$ if and only if its
Jacobian is symmetric everywhere. Writing $\sym M = (M + M^\top)/2$ and
$\asym M = (M - M^\top)/2$, and noting that these are orthogonal in the Frobenius
inner product, define
\begin{equation}
  \kappa(M) \;=\; \frac{\lVert \sym M \rVert_F}{\lVert M \rVert_F},
  \qquad \kappa = 1 \iff M \text{ symmetric}.
  \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
This paper measures $\kappa$ for Disco103.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item An analytic reference scale. Any bootstrap whose target reads only the
future is strictly triangular, hence has $\kappa = 1/\sqrt 2$ exactly,
independent of dimension and of every other detail (Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}).
Without it a measured $\kappa$ is uninterpretable.
\item A measurement procedure that survives three traps, each of which returns a
confidently wrong answer rather than an error (Section~\ref{sec:traps}), with an
estimator validated twice before use.
\item The measurement: $\kappa = 0.7084$--$0.7094$ over six configurations,
including the environment family Disco103 was meta-trained on. The update is not
a gradient field, and sits on the causal floor.
\item Three negative probes of what the discovered predictions carry, with a
positive control that works, bounding how strongly the negatives may be read.
\item A withdrawal section. Six interpretations we published in earlier drafts of
this work were wrong, and we say which and why. One of them is a headline result
of the second half, retracted by a control we ran ourselves.
\item A verified reduction and a negative result on top of it: the
GFlowNet-to-RL correspondence holds numerically to $10^{-16}$
(Section~\ref{sec:reduction}), and the MDP it produces defeats the discovered
rule outright, with a wiring control that isolates the MDP as the cause
(Section~\ref{sec:mdp}).
\item A measured scale for ``large residual'' in place of a picked threshold, and
a pipeline check that closes the one link the two estimator validations were
blind to (Section~\ref{sec:instrument}). Against that scale the family question
turns out to be confounded by optimisation, and we withdraw our own answer to it
(Section~\ref{sec:gap}).
\item A positive result of the same size as the negative ones: one scalar in the
meta-objective, chosen by the reduction rather than by search, is worth a factor
of $26$ in held-out distribution accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:scalar}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{The field and where its asymmetry lives}

DiscoRL's per-step agent loss is a sum of categorical Kullback--Leibler terms
between meta-network targets and agent predictions,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_\theta = c_\pi \KL(\hat\pi \Vert \pi_\theta)
  + c_y \KL(\hat y \Vert y_\theta)
  + c_z \KL(\hat z \Vert z_\theta(\cdot, a)) + \cdots
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with $y$ and $z$ carrying no predefined semantics. In the released
implementation $\hat\pi, \hat y, \hat z$ are wrapped in a stop-gradient when the
agent update is taken, so the update is a \emph{semi}-gradient.

We work in prediction space rather than parameter space. This is legitimate and
also convenient. If a field is a gradient in prediction space it pulls back to a
gradient in parameter space, since $-(\partial u / \partial\theta)^\top \nabla_u
\Phi = -\nabla_\theta \Phi(u(\theta))$, so the same question is being asked; and
prediction space needs only \emph{first} derivatives of the target map, which
matters for reasons given in Section~\ref{sec:traps}.

Write $u$ for the prediction logits and $p = \operatorname{softmax}(u)$. The
gradient of a categorical KL with respect to the prediction logits, target held
fixed, is the familiar difference of probabilities, so the field is
\begin{equation}
  v(u) \;=\; \hat p(u) - p(u), \qquad
  J \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{\partial \hat p}{\partial u}}_{=: B}
  \;-\; \underbrace{\frac{\partial p}{\partial u}}_{=\, \mathrm{diag}(p) - pp^\top}.
  \label{eq:field}
\end{equation}
The second term is symmetric, always. Every departure from a gradient field
therefore lives in $B$, the dependence of the target on the prediction --- which
is exactly the term the stop-gradient discards. The measurement is $\kappa(B)$.

\subsection{An analytic floor}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:floor}
For any square $M$,
$\lVert M \pm M^\top\rVert_F^2 = 2\lVert M\rVert_F^2 \pm 2\tr(M^2)$, hence
\begin{equation}
  \kappa(M)^2 \;=\; \tfrac12 \;+\; \frac{\tr(M^2)}{2\lVert M \rVert_F^2}.
  \label{eq:kappa-tr}
\end{equation}
If $M$ is strictly triangular in some ordering then $M^2$ is strictly triangular,
so $\tr(M^2) = 0$ and $\kappa(M) = 1/\sqrt 2$ exactly.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\langle M, M^\top\rangle_F = \tr(M^\top M^\top) = \tr((M^2)^\top) = \tr(M^2)$,
and expanding $\lVert M \pm M^\top \rVert_F^2$ gives the identity.
Equation~\eqref{eq:kappa-tr} follows from
$\kappa^2 = \lVert M + M^\top\rVert^2 / (\lVert M + M^\top\rVert^2 + \lVert M -
M^\top\rVert^2)$. Strict triangularity gives a nilpotent $M$ with zero diagonal,
so $\tr(M^2) = \sum_{ij} M_{ij}M_{ji} = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
A \emph{causal} bootstrap --- one whose target at index $t$ depends only on
indices after $t$ --- is strictly triangular in the time ordering. So
$\kappa = 1/\sqrt 2 \approx 0.70711$ is the structural floor imposed by the arrow
of time in credit assignment, and it is dimension-free. Tabular TD is the
canonical instance: its target map is $\hat V = r + \gamma P V$, so $B = \gamma P$
and on any acyclic graph $\kappa = 1/\sqrt 2$.
\end{remark}

The floor is not forced for DiscoRL. Its meta-network receives its own $y$ at
index $t$ as an input --- the released input specification pairs $y_t$ with
$y_{t+1}$ --- so $B$ has a nonzero diagonal block and $\tr(B^2) \neq 0$ in
general. Whether Disco103 nonetheless sits on the floor is an empirical question,
and by \eqref{eq:kappa-tr} the excess over the floor is measurable directly as
$\tr(B^2)/\lVert B\rVert_F^2 = 2\kappa^2 - 1$.

\subsection{Estimator}

Frobenius norms come from paired Hutchinson probes: for $w \sim \mathcal N(0, I)$,
$\lVert M \rVert_F^2 = \E_w \lVert M w\rVert^2$, with $Bw$ from a
Jacobian-vector product and $B^\top w$ from a vector-Jacobian product \emph{on the
same} $w$. Pairing matters: the two norms are strongly correlated, so their ratio
is far better conditioned than either term. Standard errors come from a block
jackknife over probes.

\paragraph{Validation, twice, before use.}
On synthetic fields $g(x) = (S + cA)x$ with $S$ symmetric and $A$ antisymmetric,
$\kappa$ is known in closed form; sweeping $c$ walks the estimator across
$\kappa \in [0.025, 1]$ with worst absolute error $0.0036$, and it returns
\emph{exactly} $1.0000$ at $c = 0$. Since the answer lands near $1/\sqrt 2$, we
calibrate again there: strictly triangular $B_0$ plus a controlled diagonal block,
where \eqref{eq:kappa-tr} gives the exact value, with worst error $0.0041$. That
$0.0041$ is the resolution with which any excess over the floor can be claimed,
and we quote it whenever we compare against the floor.

\section{Three traps}\label{sec:traps}

Each of the following returns a plausible number rather than an error, and each
was hit before it was fixed.

\paragraph{Second-order stop-gradient.} \texttt{jax.lax.stop\_gradient} has zero
derivative at \emph{all} orders. Differentiating the released
\texttt{backprop=False} path a second time therefore erases $B$ --- the very
object under measurement --- and reports a perfect gradient field. Our first
attempt returned $\beta = 0.0000$ from an autodiff path for exactly this reason.
The cure here is structural: $B$ is obtained as a \emph{first} derivative of the
target map, so no stop-gradient is ever on the path.

\paragraph{Querying an untrained agent.} Disco103 was meta-learned against
competent agents. Presented with a freshly initialised one --- near-uniform
policy, near-uniform $600$-way predictions --- its outputs collapse towards a
constant and every sensitivity reads as zero for reasons unrelated to
conservativity. The agent must first be trained \emph{by Disco103 itself}.

\paragraph{Silent out-of-bounds.} JAX drops out-of-bounds scatter indices
silently. A control that perturbs index $t+3$ while reading index $t$ becomes a
no-op at the end of a rollout, and the resulting $0/0$ surfaces as \texttt{nan}
rather than as an error. Relatedly, an $\varepsilon$ placed outside rather than
inside the square root in an unrolled Adam gives an infinite derivative at zero
second moment --- which masked-out logits always have --- turning the outer
gradient into \texttt{nan} while the forward pass stays finite.

\section{Result}

We train an agent with Disco103 on a given environment, freeze a rollout, and
measure $\kappa(B)$ on the joint prediction vector of policy logits, $y$ logits,
and $z$ logits at the action taken; the dimension is $86{,}616$. Two
environments are used. The first is a hypergrid, a DAG in which several action
sequences reach the same state. The second is Catch, which belongs to the family
Disco103 was actually meta-trained against and therefore answers the obvious
objection that the first is an out-of-distribution query. Training length is
swept over a factor of sixteen.

Table~\ref{tab:kappa} gives the result. Two readings, both of which matter.

\paragraph{$\kappa \neq 1$: the update is not a gradient field.} The estimator
returns exactly $1.0000$ for a genuine gradient, and the frozen-target control on
the same network --- the same loss with the target held constant, which is an
honest scalar objective --- also returns $1.0000$. Against that, $0.7088$ is not
a near miss. Disco103's update is not $-\nabla\Phi$ for any $\Phi$, so
\emph{which objective does DiscoRL minimise} is malformed rather than open.

\paragraph{$\kappa \approx 1/\sqrt2$: no more circulation than causality forces.}
Every excess over the floor lies between $+0.0013$ and $+0.0023$, below the
$0.0041$ resolution demonstrated in that band. The non-conservativity is
therefore not something meta-learning discovered; it is the generic price of a
causal bootstrap. The excess is nonzero and increases monotonically with training
length in both environments, consistent with the diagonal block that the paired
$(y_t, y_{t+1})$ input creates, but it remains within resolution throughout.

\paragraph{How much of the update is this?} $\kappa(B)$ is a property of the
bootstrap term, not of the update. The field the agent follows is
$v = \hat p - p$, so $J = B - D$ with $D$ symmetric, and $D$ is much the larger
of the two. Measuring all three operators on the same probes gives
$\kappa(D) = 1.0000$, as it must; $\lVert B\rVert_F / \lVert J \rVert_F =
0.0110$; and therefore an antisymmetric share of the whole update of
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\lVert \asym J\rVert_F}{\lVert J \rVert_F}
  = \sqrt{1 - \kappa(B)^2}\;\frac{\lVert B\rVert_F}{\lVert J\rVert_F}
  = 0.77\%.
\end{equation}
Both statements are true and they are not the same statement. The bootstrap term
is half circulation; the bootstrap term is one percent of the update. A reader
who takes $\kappa(B) = 0.709$ to mean ``the update is half non-conservative'' is
wrong by two orders of magnitude, and we would rather supply the second number
than rely on the first being read carefully.

\paragraph{What survives, and what deflates.} The qualitative claim is
untouched: $0.77\%$ is not zero, so no potential exists, no Lyapunov function is
guaranteed, and the search for an implicit objective is a search for something
that is not there. What deflates is the expected magnitude of any consequence.
A field within one percent of conservative, with a strongly contracting
symmetric part, is a small perturbation of a gradient flow, and one should
predict a correspondingly small behavioural effect. We regard this as the more
useful pair of facts: the diagnosis is structurally sound \emph{and}
quantitatively bounded. Reporting only $\kappa(B)$ would have made a
one-percent effect sound like a fifty-percent one.

\paragraph{On the Hodge projection.} By orthogonality of $\sym$ and $\asym$,
the projection onto the conservative sector retains $\kappa^2 \approx 0.502$ of
the \emph{bootstrap operator's} Frobenius mass, and $99.994\%$ of the update
field's. Neither number has been connected to sample efficiency or solution
quality, which is work we have not done.

\section{What do the discovered predictions represent?}

DiscoRL's own analysis reports that the learned predictions track future policy
entropy and upcoming large-reward events. On a hypergrid the natural candidate
state functions are computable exactly by dynamic programming, so the claim can
be tested rather than paraphrased. We use Disco103's \emph{own} scalar readout of
its $600$-way prediction --- the \texttt{y\_net} and \texttt{z\_net} multilayer
perceptrons whose weights ship with the released parameters --- so the probe has
zero free parameters. The categorical $q$ head, a value head by construction,
serves as positive control.

Table~\ref{tab:probe} reports the outcome. The control passes cleanly, so the
bucketing, the training and the dynamic programming all work; against that
baseline the $y$ channel tracks neither a flow, nor a value, nor future policy
entropy. A joint regression does not rescue it: adding $\varphi(y)$ to
$\varphi(z)$ buys $0.010$ of $R^2$ on $\log F$ but $0.060$ on the pure value
control, so it behaves as a generic weak regressor rather than as the
complementary half of a free-energy decomposition.

\paragraph{How strongly may these nulls be read?} A potential must be a state
function, and the agent's torso is recurrent, so we decompose each readout's
variance. $\varphi(y)$ is a \emph{pure} state function, $\eta^2 = 1.0000$ --- the
bucketing was never washing it out --- but its dynamic range is $68\times$
smaller than $\varphi(z)$'s. The honest scope is therefore: in this regime the
$y$ channel carries a deterministic but vanishingly small state signal that
matches no candidate potential. That is weaker than ``$y$ can never be a flow''.

\paragraph{A sensitivity that is real.} A separate probe shows the $y$-target does
respond to the log-probability of the action taken, at $139\times$ the
random-initialisation null, and the response is local: moving the perturbation
three steps later reduces it by $23\times$. The $y$ channel is not inert as a map;
it is unidentified as a function.

\section{The reduction, verified}\label{sec:reduction}

Everything from here concerns a second question, and the first answer is what
makes it worth asking. If \emph{which objective does this rule minimise} is
malformed, one can still ask what a discovered rule does in a setting where the
objective is fixed in advance and known exactly.

Tiapkin et al.\ \cite{tiapkin2024} supply that setting. Their Theorem 1: for a
DAG with terminal set $\mathcal X$, a fixed backward policy $P_B$ and a reward
$R$, build the MDP whose interior transitions pay $\log P_B(s \mid s')$ and whose
termination pays $\log R(x)$. At entropy coefficient \emph{exactly} $1$ the
soft-optimal policy is the GFlowNet forward policy and $V^*(s) = \log F(s)$.
Their Proposition 1 gives the entropy-regularised return at the source in closed
form, $V_1^\pi(s_0) = \log Z - \KL(q^\pi \Vert P_B)$, with supremum $\log Z$.

We derive none of this. We check it, on a hypergrid, against an exact dynamic
program.

\centering

\midrule

R2 & $\lvert V^*(s_0) - \log Z\rvert$ & $0$ exactly \\
R3 & $\max_x \lvert p(x) - R(x)/Z\rvert$ & $2.776\times 10^{-17}$ \\
R4 & same, over $50$ random backward policies & worst $1.665\times 10^{-16}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The reduction checked numerically on a hypergrid. R4 is the statement
that the backward policy is a gauge freedom, exercised rather than argued.}
\label{tab:reduction}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:reduction} is the result. The coefficient is not a free parameter,
and Remark 3 of the same paper says what moving it costs. Pricing that remark
here, as the KL of the terminal distribution to $R/Z$, gives $\lambda = 0.9
\to 0.0367$, $1.0 \to -6.5\times 10^{-17}$, $1.1 \to 0.0386$, $2.0 \to 1.134$
and $4.0 \to 1.782$. The reduction is exact at one and at no other coefficient,
and the price of being wrong about it grows quickly.

This settles a question of well-posedness rather than of performance. Because the
correspondence is exact, ``meta-learn a GFlowNet update rule'' names a concrete
MDP with a concrete entropy coefficient, and a rule can be dropped into it and
scored. The rest of the paper does that.

\section{The MDP the reduction produces}\label{sec:mdp}

The first thing to record is that Disco103 has no entropy coefficient to set. Its
\texttt{hyper\us params} are \texttt{pi\us cost}, \texttt{y\us cost},
\texttt{z\us cost}, \texttt{value\us cost}, \texttt{aux\us policy\us cost},
\texttt{target\us params\us coeff}, \texttt{value\us fn\us td\us lambda} and
\texttt{discount}. The actor-critic in the same library carries an
\texttt{entropy\us cost} of $0.2$; the discovered rule carries no such knob. This
is verified in source, not read off the paper.

The environment is a $6\times 6$ grid with three smooth reward modes, $\log R$
spanning $5.43$. The best undiscounted return available is $2.285$ with the
$\log P_B$ payments in place and $3.431$ without them. Stopping at the source pays
$-2.000$.

\paragraph{The failure is the MDP, not the wiring.} The unshaped number is
identical to three decimals across all three seeds, which is already a hint that
nothing stochastic is happening. The middle row of Table~\ref{tab:mdp} settles it:
the same rule, the same network, the same observation and the same budget solve
the same grid to a gap of $0.552$ once the per-step payments are removed. That
row is a control on the harness and nothing else. It is not an arm at some other
temperature, and its residual would not mean anything, so we do not report one.

\paragraph{Mechanism.} Each interior step pays about $-0.69$. Stopping
immediately therefore returns $-2.000$, which is a genuine improvement over the
initial policy's $-2.281$, and escaping that basin requires a specific five-step
path. The rule takes the improvement and stays. This is Remark 4 of
\cite{tiapkin2024} --- soft actor-critic with an adaptive coefficient converging
to a biased final policy --- reproduced for a discovered rule, which has no
coefficient to adapt in the first place.

\section{Shaping}\label{sec:shaping}

The basin is an optimisation obstacle, and potential shaping is the standard
instrument for one. Let $\Phi$ be the soft value at coefficient $1$ of the same
DAG with the reward flattened away. This matters and is the whole reason the
section exists: $\Phi$ is pure graph geometry and never reads $R$, so it cannot
be the answer smuggled in as a hint.

For the shaped reward $r' = r + \Phi(s') - \Phi(s)$,
\begin{align}
  \lambda \log \sum_a \exp\!\big((r' + V'(s'))/\lambda\big) &= V(s) - \Phi(s),
  \label{eq:shape-value}\\
  \pi' \;\propto\; \exp\!\big((r + V(s'))/\lambda\big) &= \pi .
  \label{eq:shape-policy}
\end{align}
The value function shifts by $\Phi$ and the soft-optimal policy does not move.

We checked this pointwise rather than resting on the algebra. The maximum change
in the soft-optimal policy over $\lambda \in \{0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, 5\}$ is
$1.776\times 10^{-15}$. So the third row of Table~\ref{tab:mdp} is the same
target reached from a better-conditioned starting point, and the gap it closes,
$4.285$ down to $0.323$, is entirely optimisation.

\section{The instrument}\label{sec:instrument}

Asking whether the resulting sampler is a tempered one requires an instrument,
and the instrument has to be trusted before its readings are. The soft family is
computed exactly by value iteration; an agent is bucketed into a
state-conditional policy; the coefficient is fitted by golden section against
exactly recomputed members. The fit returns a coefficient \emph{and} the residual
divergence that no coefficient removes, and it is the second number that was
supposed to decide family membership.

\paragraph{The bucketing check carries no sampling noise.} On a DAG whose
trajectories all begin at the source,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{reach}(c) \;=\; \sum_{p} \frac{n(p)}{N}\,\frac{n(p \to c)}{n(p)}
  \;=\; \frac{n(c)}{N},
  \label{eq:bucket}
\end{equation}
so the forward dynamic program run under the bucketed policy reproduces the
empirical visit frequencies identically. The $10^{-17}$ in
Table~\ref{tab:instrument} is therefore not a small statistical fluctuation that
happened to come out small; any departure from zero would measure dependence on
history alone.

agents act in the \texttt{disco\_rl} environment and their rollouts are handed
over wholesale, including rollout-boundary partial episodes, which the exact
sampler used in I1 and I2 never produces.
\texttt{temperature\_pipeline\_check.py} closes it by driving the exact
soft-optimal policy through that same environment path and asking the readout
what it sees: $\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.995$ at residual $0.0062$. The pipeline
is sound, so the residuals the arms show are theirs.

\section{The misfit is the optimisation gap}\label{sec:gap}

\paragraph{The ladder was never a calibration.} \texttt{temperature.py}
calibrated its readout against actor-critic arms at \texttt{entropy\_cost} $c \in
\{0, 0.3, 1, 3\}$, and an earlier version of that file described the arm at
$c = 1$ as ``an arm whose coefficient is exactly one by construction''. That is
false, and seeing it requires no training run at all: the exact fixed point of
that update rule is $\pi \propto \exp(Q_\pi / c)$ with the \emph{ordinary}
$Q_\pi$, which is a different one-parameter family. It coincides with the soft
family below $c \approx 0.5$ and leaves it above, reaching residual $3.02$ at
$c = 3$.

The fixed point predicts arms it was never fitted to. At $c = 3$ it gives
residual $3.021$ against the measured $3.009$, and $\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}$
$1.636$ against the measured $1.639$. At $c = 1$ it sits at
$\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.836$ with residual $0.685$, eight times the ceiling
of Table~\ref{tab:scale}. No rung of the ladder pinned the absolute scale; the
ladder ranks, it does not calibrate.

Computing the $c$-to-$\lambda$ map by dynamic programming repairs the reading.
The map is monotone and invertible, and through it the shaped arm's aggregate
$0.362$ is an equivalent entropy coefficient of $0.418$, against the $1.181$ that
would place an arm at $\lambda = 1$. The map is what allows a coefficient in one
family to be compared with a coefficient in the other at all. The aggregate it
converts, however, does not survive what follows.

\paragraph{A verdict we withdraw.} The soft family answers ``is it a GFlowNet''.
The fixed-point family above answers ``is it an entropy-bonus actor-critic''. We
fitted both to the shaped arm and reported the means over final-step admissible
rows: soft $0.6482$ and fixed-point $0.3720$ at three seeds, soft $0.4734$ and
fixed-point $0.2977$ at six. All four are above the $0.0861$ ceiling, and we read
that as \emph{neither family}. The reading is withdrawn. The per-seed rows behind
those means run from $0.041$ to $1.423$, and that range is not noise.

\section{One scalar in the meta-objective}\label{sec:scalar}

The sections above are diagnostic, and they share a shape: a discovered rule is
scored against a target it was never given. Proposition 1 of \cite{tiapkin2024}
says the target is available cheaply, so the last experiment gives it to the
meta-learner and changes nothing else.

\paragraph{The identity, checked first.} In double precision, on the shaped
rewards, $V_1 + \Phi(s_0) = \log Z$ to $0.00\mathrm{e}{+}00$, and the policy
attaining it has divergence $5\times 10^{-16}$. The entropy-regularised return in
this MDP \emph{is} distribution accuracy, at the price of a scalar computed from
trajectories. Nothing about the rule needs to know that.

\paragraph{The rule is local.} Per transition it reads the immediate reward, its
own auxiliary prediction at both ends, the current logit and log-probability, and
whether the action terminates. It never sees $R/Z$, $\log Z$ or the visit
distribution. Everything downstream of the policy is closed form --- the grid is
a layered DAG, so occupancy is $e_0^\top (I - T)^{-1}$ --- which removes sampling
noise from the meta-gradient. What is reported is therefore the infinite-sample
limit of the same rule. Meta-training uses $16$ random landscapes and the readout
is on $16$ disjoint ones.

\paragraph{Two controls.} First, each arm chooses its own step sizes, scored by
\emph{its own} meta-objective on training landscapes and never by divergence to
the target. Second, each rule must win on the scalar it was trained on. It does,
at the trained horizon and four times beyond: on the return the return-trained
rule scores $-0.341$ against $-1.327$, and on $V_1$ the $V_1$-trained rule scores
$+2.231$ against $-0.298$. An earlier run of this comparison showed an $88\times$
separation. The second control voided it --- the return-trained rule was losing
on return itself, so that run compared a trained rule against an undertrained one
--- and that is a withdrawal (Section~\ref{sec:withdrawals}).

about exploration.

\section{Withdrawals}\label{sec:withdrawals}

An earlier draft of this work reported two interpretations that we now retract.
Both arose from the same failure: reading a number that has a simple structural
explanation as evidence for a more elaborate one.

First, we reported the ratio of the $y$-target's sensitivity to log-policy against
its sensitivity to the successor prediction, $|\beta/\alpha| = 0.32$, as ``a third
of the way to detailed balance''. That reading presumes $\varphi(y)$ is a
log-flow quantity. The calibration in Table~\ref{tab:probe} refutes the premise,
so the reading is withdrawn; the sensitivity measurement itself stands.

Second, we read $\kappa^2 \approx 0.5$ as evidence that the update implements a
saddle-point, primal--dual scheme, on the grounds that such schemes have an
irreducibly antisymmetric coupling block. But Proposition~\ref{prop:floor} already
accounts for the same number with no saddle structure whatsoever, and it was
derived first. The simpler explanation suffices; the saddle reading is withdrawn.

Four further retractions belong to the second half of this work, and the last is
the largest.

Third, the residual threshold $0.4$ in \texttt{temperature.py}, above which an
agent was declared outside the soft family, was picked and not measured. It is
replaced by $0.0861$, the cost of a $25\%$ temperature error, which is measured
(Table~\ref{tab:scale}).

Fourth, \texttt{temperature.py} treated the actor-critic \texttt{entropy\_cost}
as the soft family's coefficient, and an earlier version of that file called the
arm at $c = 1$ an arm whose coefficient is one by construction. It is not: the
fixed point is $\pi \propto \exp(Q_\pi / c)$ with the ordinary $Q_\pi$, a
different family (Section~\ref{sec:gap}). The ladder ranks, it does not
calibrate, and the calibration reading is withdrawn.

Fifth, we reported an $88\times$ separation between the two meta-objectives. Its
own control voided it: the return-trained rule was losing on return, so the
comparison was between a trained rule and an undertrained one. The separation we
stand behind is the factor of $26$ in Table~\ref{tab:meta}.

Sixth, we reported a verdict of \emph{neither family} for the shaped arm, from
the means of the family comparison. The residual it rested on is predicted by the
optimisation gap at $R^2$ $0.993$ and extrapolates to $0.0410$ nats at zero gap,
inside the measured ceiling, so it was never evidence about family membership
(Section~\ref{sec:gap}). The verdict is withdrawn. The reproducer is
\texttt{research/temperature\_gap\_confound.py}. What replaces it is weaker and
is stated as such: at the best-optimising seed the arm is inside both families,
and this MDP does not separate them.

The withdrawals of the earlier draft stand as well: the gauge theorem conceded to
Brunswic et al., the detailed-balance reading of $|\beta/\alpha|$, the saddle
reading of $\kappa^2$, and the Hatano--Sasa equality demoted to a structural
isomorphism.

\section{Related work, and what is not ours}

The gradient-field question for bootstrapped updates is old. Residual-gradient
methods differentiate through the target and are genuine gradient methods, at the
cost of a double-sampling bias in stochastic environments and a different, often
worse, fixed point under function approximation \cite{baird1995residual}; the
gradient-TD family restores a true objective by introducing an auxiliary variable
\cite{sutton2009gtd}, and the convex-duality view organises this
\cite{nachum2020fenchel}. What is new here is not the question but its
application to a rule that was discovered rather than designed, together with an
analytic reference scale that makes the answer readable.

We also record what is \emph{not} ours. In the course of this work we derived a
structural result about GFlowNet flow spaces --- that the set of $R$-flows is an
affine set directed by the cycle space of the state graph, so the backward policy
is a gauge freedom that cannot move the terminal distribution. It is published.
Brunswic et al.\ \cite{brunswic2024nonacyclic} prove $\mathcal F_R = F + H^1(G)$
and attribute a stronger form to Kalpazidou; that every backward policy induces
the same terminal distribution is standard and stated in at least six papers,
most crisply in trajectory balance \cite{malkin2022tb}, which already names
cycles in the underlying undirected graph as the source of the multiplicity;
and learning the backward policy for performance with correctness preserved by
construction has been done with at least five distinct objectives. Our addition
was the dimension $|E| - |V| + 1$ and its identification with the parameter count
of the backward policy, which is the first Betti number and follows immediately.
We claim none of it.

We note also that the method we had been using to judge novelty --- counting
arXiv abstract matches --- failed on exactly this case, because the pre-empting
paper writes $H^1(G)$ in cohomological notation and never uses the phrase
``cycle space''.

\section{Limitations}

The measurement is of one rule, Disco103, on two environments. Catch answers the
distribution objection but neither environment is ProcGen or DMLab. $\kappa$ is
computed on a frozen rollout at a single point in parameter space, and while it is
stable across a sixteen-fold range of training length, it is a local quantity.
The probe restricts to the prediction channels the agent loss touches and holds
the Polyak-delayed target network fixed, which is faithful within one update but
suppresses one dependency. Batch elements couple at $O(1/B)$ through the
advantage and TD normalisers. Finally, both conservative-mass statements --- $\kappa(B)^2 \approx 0.5$ for the
bootstrap term and a $0.77\%$ antisymmetric share for the update --- are
operator-level facts with no demonstrated behavioural consequence. The natural
next step is the counterfactual: let the gradient flow through the target as
well, which makes the update conservative by construction, and measure what
performance changes. The magnitudes above predict that little will.

The second half of the paper carries its own bounds. It is a $6\times 6$ grid
with a tabular policy and exact expectations; Section~\ref{sec:scalar} is an
infinite-sample limit and not a sampled algorithm. That section compares against
detailed balance only, and loses to it asymptotically. Its seed spread is large
relative to its mean. The positive reading in Section~\ref{sec:gap} rests on the
single best-optimising admissible seed plus an extrapolation to zero gap; it is
not three independent seeds at zero gap. And all of it is one reward family on
one graph topology.

\section{Reproducibility}

Every number in this paper is produced by a script, and the scripts are in the
accompanying repository. \texttt{kappa.py} performs the estimator validation and
the measurement; \texttt{calibrate.py} and \texttt{thermo.py} perform the
prediction probes and the variance decomposition; \texttt{disco\_probe.py}
performs the sensitivity measurement; \texttt{verify\_disco\_source.py} encodes
the claims we make about the released implementation as executable assertions
pinned to commit \texttt{9059a29f}, re-run weekly, so that a claim about someone
else's code cannot rot silently. All exact quantities are computed by dynamic
programming over the state graph rather than sampled.

The second half adds nine. \texttt{tiapkin.py} checks R1--R4 of the reduction
and prices Remark 3. \texttt{temperature.py} builds the reduction MDP, runs the
three arms of Table~\ref{tab:mdp} and fits the soft-family coefficient.
\texttt{temperature\_pipeline\_check.py} drives the exact soft-optimal policy
through the agents' own environment path, returning $\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}} =
0.995$ at residual $0.0062$, which is the evidence that the readout's residuals
belong to the arms. \texttt{temperature\_residual\_scale.py} measures what a
temperature error costs and is where $0.0861$ comes from.
\texttt{temperature\_rule\_fixedpoint.py} computes the actor-critic rule's exact
fixed point and the $c$-to-$\lambda$ map, and is what shows the ladder never
calibrated. \texttt{temperature\_family\_compare.py} fits both families to the
same arm at three and six seeds. \texttt{temperature\_gap\_confound.py} rereads
its per-seed rows against the optimisation gap and is what withdrew our family
verdict. \texttt{temperature\_critic\_ablation.py} swaps the value head and
excludes value resolution. \texttt{metagfn.py} meta-learns the local update rule
under each scalar and runs the two controls and the detailed-balance comparison.
