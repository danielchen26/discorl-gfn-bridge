\title{\bf Does a Discovered Reinforcement Learning Rule\\ Minimise Anything?\\[2mm]
\large A gradient-field audit of DiscoRL}

\author{Tianchi Chen}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
DiscoRL replaces a hand-written reinforcement learning update with a
meta-learned one, and the meta-parameters of its strongest rule, Disco103, are
public. That makes a question askable that could not be asked of a rule nobody
had written down: \emph{is the discovered update the gradient of anything?} The
question is not rhetorical. By the Poincar\'e lemma a vector field is a gradient
exactly when its Jacobian is symmetric, so ``which objective does this rule
minimise'' is well posed only if a single scalar, $\kappa = \lVert \sym
J\rVert_F / \lVert J \rVert_F$, equals one.

We measure it. Working in prediction space rather than parameter space, the
non-conservative part of the field is isolated in the bootstrap operator
$B = \partial \hat p / \partial u$ --- precisely the term \texttt{stop\_gradient}
discards --- and we estimate $\kappa(B)$ by paired Hutchinson probes through a
single forward map. Two things make the number readable. First, an analytic
reference: a \emph{causal} bootstrap, whose target reads only the future, is
strictly triangular, so $\tr(B^2) = 0$ and $\kappa = 1/\sqrt 2$ exactly, in any
dimension. Second, an estimator validated before it is believed, on synthetic
fields spanning $\kappa \in [0.025, 1]$ and again in the narrow band where the
answer lands, and returning exactly $1.0000$ for a genuine gradient.

Across six configurations --- two environments, one of them from Disco103's own
meta-training family, and training lengths spanning a factor of sixteen ---
$\kappa = 0.7084$ to $0.7094$, against a floor of $0.70711$. Disco103's update
is therefore not the gradient of any functional, and it carries no more
circulation than causal bootstrapping forces. Three probes of what the
discovered predictions represent all return nothing, against a positive control
that works. We report the traps that silently return the wrong answer, and two
claims of our own that we withdrew.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A reinforcement learning algorithm is usually an equation. DiscoRL
\cite{oh2025disco} replaces the equation with a network: a meta-network reads a
trajectory and emits targets, the agent regresses onto them, and the
meta-network itself is trained by meta-gradient across a population of agents.
The resulting rule, Disco103, matches or beats hand-designed algorithms on
Atari, ProcGen and DMLab, and generalises to environments never seen during
discovery. Its meta-parameters ship under an open licence.

That artefact makes a structural question concrete. Hand-designed rules come
with a story about what they optimise --- policy gradient ascends expected
return, trajectory balance \cite{malkin2022tb} descends an explicit residual.
A discovered rule comes with no such story. It is natural to ask which objective
it implicitly minimises, and a good deal of theory would follow if one could
name it.

The question presupposes that an objective exists. It need not. Temporal
difference learning famously is not the gradient of any function: the target is
held fixed when the gradient is taken, and the discarded term is what a genuine
gradient would have included \cite{baird1995residual,sutton2009gtd}. DiscoRL is
built the same way --- produce a target, regress onto it --- so the
presupposition has to be tested rather than assumed.

By the Poincar\'e lemma the test reduces to one scalar. On a simply connected
domain a smooth field $v$ is $-\nabla \Phi$ for some $\Phi$ if and only if its
Jacobian is symmetric everywhere. Writing $\sym M = (M + M^\top)/2$ and
$\asym M = (M - M^\top)/2$, and noting that these are orthogonal in the Frobenius
inner product, define
\begin{equation}
  \kappa(M) \;=\; \frac{\lVert \sym M \rVert_F}{\lVert M \rVert_F},
  \qquad \kappa = 1 \iff M \text{ symmetric}.
  \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
This paper measures $\kappa$ for Disco103.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item An analytic reference scale. Any bootstrap whose target reads only the
future is strictly triangular, hence has $\kappa = 1/\sqrt 2$ exactly,
independent of dimension and of every other detail (Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}).
Without it a measured $\kappa$ is uninterpretable.
\item A measurement procedure that survives three traps, each of which returns a
confidently wrong answer rather than an error (Section~\ref{sec:traps}), with an
estimator validated twice before use.
\item The measurement: $\kappa = 0.7084$--$0.7094$ over six configurations,
including the environment family Disco103 was meta-trained on. The update is not
a gradient field, and sits on the causal floor.
\item Three negative probes of what the discovered predictions carry, with a
positive control that works, bounding how strongly the negatives may be read.
\item A withdrawal section. Two interpretations we published in an earlier draft
of this work were wrong, and we say which and why.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The field and where its asymmetry lives}

DiscoRL's per-step agent loss is a sum of categorical Kullback--Leibler terms
between meta-network targets and agent predictions,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_\theta = c_\pi \KL(\hat\pi \Vert \pi_\theta)
  + c_y \KL(\hat y \Vert y_\theta)
  + c_z \KL(\hat z \Vert z_\theta(\cdot, a)) + \cdots
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with $y$ and $z$ carrying no predefined semantics. In the released
implementation $\hat\pi, \hat y, \hat z$ are wrapped in a stop-gradient when the
agent update is taken, so the update is a \emph{semi}-gradient.

We work in prediction space rather than parameter space. This is legitimate and
also convenient. If a field is a gradient in prediction space it pulls back to a
gradient in parameter space, since $-(\partial u / \partial\theta)^\top \nabla_u
\Phi = -\nabla_\theta \Phi(u(\theta))$, so the same question is being asked; and
prediction space needs only \emph{first} derivatives of the target map, which
matters for reasons given in Section~\ref{sec:traps}.

Write $u$ for the prediction logits and $p = \operatorname{softmax}(u)$. The
gradient of a categorical KL with respect to the prediction logits, target held
fixed, is the familiar difference of probabilities, so the field is
\begin{equation}
  v(u) \;=\; \hat p(u) - p(u), \qquad
  J \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{\partial \hat p}{\partial u}}_{=: B}
  \;-\; \underbrace{\frac{\partial p}{\partial u}}_{=\, \mathrm{diag}(p) - pp^\top}.
  \label{eq:field}
\end{equation}
The second term is symmetric, always. Every departure from a gradient field
therefore lives in $B$, the dependence of the target on the prediction --- which
is exactly the term the stop-gradient discards. The measurement is $\kappa(B)$.

\subsection{An analytic floor}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:floor}
For any square $M$,
$\lVert M \pm M^\top\rVert_F^2 = 2\lVert M\rVert_F^2 \pm 2\tr(M^2)$, hence
\begin{equation}
  \kappa(M)^2 \;=\; \tfrac12 \;+\; \frac{\tr(M^2)}{2\lVert M \rVert_F^2}.
  \label{eq:kappa-tr}
\end{equation}
If $M$ is strictly triangular in some ordering then $M^2$ is strictly triangular,
so $\tr(M^2) = 0$ and $\kappa(M) = 1/\sqrt 2$ exactly.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\langle M, M^\top\rangle_F = \tr(M^\top M^\top) = \tr((M^2)^\top) = \tr(M^2)$,
and expanding $\lVert M \pm M^\top \rVert_F^2$ gives the identity.
Equation~\eqref{eq:kappa-tr} follows from
$\kappa^2 = \lVert M + M^\top\rVert^2 / (\lVert M + M^\top\rVert^2 + \lVert M -
M^\top\rVert^2)$. Strict triangularity gives a nilpotent $M$ with zero diagonal,
so $\tr(M^2) = \sum_{ij} M_{ij}M_{ji} = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
A \emph{causal} bootstrap --- one whose target at index $t$ depends only on
indices after $t$ --- is strictly triangular in the time ordering. So
$\kappa = 1/\sqrt 2 \approx 0.70711$ is the structural floor imposed by the arrow
of time in credit assignment, and it is dimension-free. Tabular TD is the
canonical instance: its target map is $\hat V = r + \gamma P V$, so $B = \gamma P$
and on any acyclic graph $\kappa = 1/\sqrt 2$.
\end{remark}

The floor is not forced for DiscoRL. Its meta-network receives its own $y$ at
index $t$ as an input --- the released input specification pairs $y_t$ with
$y_{t+1}$ --- so $B$ has a nonzero diagonal block and $\tr(B^2) \neq 0$ in
general. Whether Disco103 nonetheless sits on the floor is an empirical question,
and by \eqref{eq:kappa-tr} the excess over the floor is measurable directly as
$\tr(B^2)/\lVert B\rVert_F^2 = 2\kappa^2 - 1$.

\subsection{Estimator}

Frobenius norms come from paired Hutchinson probes: for $w \sim \mathcal N(0, I)$,
$\lVert M \rVert_F^2 = \E_w \lVert M w\rVert^2$, with $Bw$ from a
Jacobian-vector product and $B^\top w$ from a vector-Jacobian product \emph{on the
same} $w$. Pairing matters: the two norms are strongly correlated, so their ratio
is far better conditioned than either term. Standard errors come from a block
jackknife over probes.

\paragraph{Validation, twice, before use.}
On synthetic fields $g(x) = (S + cA)x$ with $S$ symmetric and $A$ antisymmetric,
$\kappa$ is known in closed form; sweeping $c$ walks the estimator across
$\kappa \in [0.025, 1]$ with worst absolute error $0.0036$, and it returns
\emph{exactly} $1.0000$ at $c = 0$. Since the answer lands near $1/\sqrt 2$, we
calibrate again there: strictly triangular $B_0$ plus a controlled diagonal block,
where \eqref{eq:kappa-tr} gives the exact value, with worst error $0.0041$. That
$0.0041$ is the resolution with which any excess over the floor can be claimed,
and we quote it whenever we compare against the floor.

\section{Three traps}\label{sec:traps}

Each of the following returns a plausible number rather than an error, and each
was hit before it was fixed.

\paragraph{Second-order stop-gradient.} \texttt{jax.lax.stop\_gradient} has zero
derivative at \emph{all} orders. Differentiating the released
\texttt{backprop=False} path a second time therefore erases $B$ --- the very
object under measurement --- and reports a perfect gradient field. Our first
attempt returned $\beta = 0.0000$ from an autodiff path for exactly this reason.
The cure here is structural: $B$ is obtained as a \emph{first} derivative of the
target map, so no stop-gradient is ever on the path.

\paragraph{Querying an untrained agent.} Disco103 was meta-learned against
competent agents. Presented with a freshly initialised one --- near-uniform
policy, near-uniform $600$-way predictions --- its outputs collapse towards a
constant and every sensitivity reads as zero for reasons unrelated to
conservativity. The agent must first be trained \emph{by Disco103 itself}.

\paragraph{Silent out-of-bounds.} JAX drops out-of-bounds scatter indices
silently. A control that perturbs index $t+3$ while reading index $t$ becomes a
no-op at the end of a rollout, and the resulting $0/0$ surfaces as \texttt{nan}
rather than as an error. Relatedly, an $\varepsilon$ placed outside rather than
inside the square root in an unrolled Adam gives an infinite derivative at zero
second moment --- which masked-out logits always have --- turning the outer
gradient into \texttt{nan} while the forward pass stays finite.

\section{Result}

We train an agent with Disco103 on a given environment, freeze a rollout, and
measure $\kappa(B)$ on the joint prediction vector of policy logits, $y$ logits,
and $z$ logits at the action taken; the dimension is $86{,}616$. Two
environments are used. The first is a hypergrid, a DAG in which several action
sequences reach the same state. The second is Catch, which belongs to the family
Disco103 was actually meta-trained against and therefore answers the obvious
objection that the first is an out-of-distribution query. Training length is
swept over a factor of sixteen.

Table~\ref{tab:kappa} gives the result. Two readings, both of which matter.

\paragraph{$\kappa \neq 1$: the update is not a gradient field.} The estimator
returns exactly $1.0000$ for a genuine gradient, and the frozen-target control on
the same network --- the same loss with the target held constant, which is an
honest scalar objective --- also returns $1.0000$. Against that, $0.7088$ is not
a near miss. Disco103's update is not $-\nabla\Phi$ for any $\Phi$, so
\emph{which objective does DiscoRL minimise} is malformed rather than open.

\paragraph{$\kappa \approx 1/\sqrt2$: no more circulation than causality forces.}
Every excess over the floor lies between $+0.0013$ and $+0.0023$, below the
$0.0041$ resolution demonstrated in that band. The non-conservativity is
therefore not something meta-learning discovered; it is the generic price of a
causal bootstrap. The excess is nonzero and increases monotonically with training
length in both environments, consistent with the diagonal block that the paired
$(y_t, y_{t+1})$ input creates, but it remains within resolution throughout.

\paragraph{What can be said about the conservative part.} By orthogonality of
$\sym$ and $\asym$, the Hodge projection onto the conservative sector retains
$\kappa^2 \approx 0.502$ of the operator's Frobenius mass. We state this at the
level of the \emph{operator} only. Connecting an operator-mass split to sample
efficiency or solution quality is work we have not done, and the number should
not be read as ``half of DiscoRL is a gradient method''.

\section{What do the discovered predictions represent?}

DiscoRL's own analysis reports that the learned predictions track future policy
entropy and upcoming large-reward events. On a hypergrid the natural candidate
state functions are computable exactly by dynamic programming, so the claim can
be tested rather than paraphrased. We use Disco103's \emph{own} scalar readout of
its $600$-way prediction --- the \texttt{y\_net} and \texttt{z\_net} multilayer
perceptrons whose weights ship with the released parameters --- so the probe has
zero free parameters. The categorical $q$ head, a value head by construction,
serves as positive control.

Table~\ref{tab:probe} reports the outcome. The control passes cleanly, so the
bucketing, the training and the dynamic programming all work; against that
baseline the $y$ channel tracks neither a flow, nor a value, nor future policy
entropy. A joint regression does not rescue it: adding $\varphi(y)$ to
$\varphi(z)$ buys $0.010$ of $R^2$ on $\log F$ but $0.060$ on the pure value
control, so it behaves as a generic weak regressor rather than as the
complementary half of a free-energy decomposition.

\paragraph{How strongly may these nulls be read?} A potential must be a state
function, and the agent's torso is recurrent, so we decompose each readout's
variance. $\varphi(y)$ is a \emph{pure} state function, $\eta^2 = 1.0000$ --- the
bucketing was never washing it out --- but its dynamic range is $68\times$
smaller than $\varphi(z)$'s. The honest scope is therefore: in this regime the
$y$ channel carries a deterministic but vanishingly small state signal that
matches no candidate potential. That is weaker than ``$y$ can never be a flow''.

\paragraph{A sensitivity that is real.} A separate probe shows the $y$-target does
respond to the log-probability of the action taken, at $139\times$ the
random-initialisation null, and the response is local: moving the perturbation
three steps later reduces it by $23\times$. The $y$ channel is not inert as a map;
it is unidentified as a function.

\section{Withdrawals}

An earlier draft of this work reported two interpretations that we now retract.
Both arose from the same failure: reading a number that has a simple structural
explanation as evidence for a more elaborate one.

First, we reported the ratio of the $y$-target's sensitivity to log-policy against
its sensitivity to the successor prediction, $|\beta/\alpha| = 0.32$, as ``a third
of the way to detailed balance''. That reading presumes $\varphi(y)$ is a
log-flow quantity. The calibration in Table~\ref{tab:probe} refutes the premise,
so the reading is withdrawn; the sensitivity measurement itself stands.

Second, we read $\kappa^2 \approx 0.5$ as evidence that the update implements a
saddle-point, primal--dual scheme, on the grounds that such schemes have an
irreducibly antisymmetric coupling block. But Proposition~\ref{prop:floor} already
accounts for the same number with no saddle structure whatsoever, and it was
derived first. The simpler explanation suffices; the saddle reading is withdrawn.

\section{Related work, and what is not ours}

The gradient-field question for bootstrapped updates is old. Residual-gradient
methods differentiate through the target and are genuine gradient methods, at the
cost of a double-sampling bias in stochastic environments and a different, often
worse, fixed point under function approximation \cite{baird1995residual}; the
gradient-TD family restores a true objective by introducing an auxiliary variable
\cite{sutton2009gtd}, and the convex-duality view organises this
\cite{nachum2020fenchel}. What is new here is not the question but its
application to a rule that was discovered rather than designed, together with an
analytic reference scale that makes the answer readable.

We also record what is \emph{not} ours. In the course of this work we derived a
structural result about GFlowNet flow spaces --- that the set of $R$-flows is an
affine set directed by the cycle space of the state graph, so the backward policy
is a gauge freedom that cannot move the terminal distribution. It is published.
Brunswic et al.\ \cite{brunswic2024nonacyclic} prove $\mathcal F_R = F + H^1(G)$
and attribute a stronger form to Kalpazidou; that every backward policy induces
the same terminal distribution is standard and stated in at least six papers,
most crisply in trajectory balance \cite{malkin2022tb}, which already names
cycles in the underlying undirected graph as the source of the multiplicity;
and learning the backward policy for performance with correctness preserved by
construction has been done with at least five distinct objectives. Our addition
was the dimension $|E| - |V| + 1$ and its identification with the parameter count
of the backward policy, which is the first Betti number and follows immediately.
We claim none of it.

We note also that the method we had been using to judge novelty --- counting
arXiv abstract matches --- failed on exactly this case, because the pre-empting
paper writes $H^1(G)$ in cohomological notation and never uses the phrase
``cycle space''.

\section{Limitations}

The measurement is of one rule, Disco103, on two environments. Catch answers the
distribution objection but neither environment is ProcGen or DMLab. $\kappa$ is
computed on a frozen rollout at a single point in parameter space, and while it is
stable across a sixteen-fold range of training length, it is a local quantity.
The probe restricts to the prediction channels the agent loss touches and holds
the Polyak-delayed target network fixed, which is faithful within one update but
suppresses one dependency. Batch elements couple at $O(1/B)$ through the
advantage and TD normalisers. Finally, the conservative-mass statement
$\kappa^2 \approx 0.5$ is an operator-level fact with no demonstrated behavioural
consequence.

\section{Reproducibility}

Every number in this paper is produced by a script, and the scripts are in the
accompanying repository. \texttt{kappa.py} performs the estimator validation and
the measurement; \texttt{calibrate.py} and \texttt{thermo.py} perform the
prediction probes and the variance decomposition; \texttt{disco\_probe.py}
performs the sensitivity measurement; \texttt{verify\_disco\_source.py} encodes
the claims we make about the released implementation as executable assertions
pinned to commit \texttt{9059a29f}, re-run weekly, so that a claim about someone
else's code cannot rot silently. All exact quantities are computed by dynamic
programming over the state graph rather than sampled.
